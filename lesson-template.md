Lesson Template — Reference Example
This is the canonical structure. When generating a new lesson, keep every section, the timings,
the task types and the teacher-facing apparatus; replace the topic, central question and the
five target expressions, and regenerate all content so every example and activity practises
the new targets through the new topic's professional domain.
Header block (always include):
·	Title: C1/C1+ PROFESSIONAL ENGLISH — LESSON [n]: [Topic]
·	Level: C1/C1+ · Duration: 2 hours · Audience: [professionals]
·	Central question: [one sentence]
·	Today's five targets: 2 phrasal verbs, 2 phrases, 1 financial expression
Section-by-section skeleton (with timings)
1.	Lesson objectives — 5–6 bullets ("By the end of the lesson, students should be able
 to…") covering: presenting a structured argument with evidence; using each target's
 function; distinguishing related functions; using the targets naturally under pressure;
 responding to a sceptical counterpart.
2.	The hook (15 min) — A scripted professional situation with a 3-turn dialogue (counterpart
 challenges, company gives a generic answer, counterpart rejects it as indistinguishable),
 8–10 numbered discussion questions, and a Teacher's intervention with a timed
 constraint banning generic promotional words.
3.	The five-way distinction — A 3-column table: Expression | Core meaning | Communication
 function. One row per target.
4.	Language lab (the five targets) — Per target: type label, Meaning, Structure, common
 combinations, 5 professional examples, 2 counterpart-facing questions, and a
 Key distinction paragraph contrasting it with the other targets and flagging how it may
 be challenged.
5.	Speaking activities (5):
o	Activity 1 — Diagnose (15 min): 5 situations, each mapping to one target; each has a
 question, teacher answer, "Why?" and a Challenge follow-up.
o	Activity 2 — Rebuild (15 min): 10 ordinary statements rewritten verbally with the
targets; each has a model answer and a one-line rationale; plus a C1+ extension demanding
evidence per reformulation.
o	Activity 3 — Precision under pressure (15 min): 5 scenarios; counterpart question,
student task, teacher model (45–60 s responses) and a pressure question each.
o	Activity 4 — Reverse Jeopardy (10 min): 8 professional answers for which students
reconstruct the question/situation; 2 possible questions per round, ≥1 round per target.
o	Activity 5 — Controlled role-play (20 min): 3 paired role-plays (counterpart line,
student task, follow-up, required language, teacher model) plus a 4th integrated
challenge (multi-part committee exchange using all five targets, with a model opening
and a teacher follow-up).
6.	Final challenge (10 min) — A timed pitch (3 minutes) with a numbered task list, a rule to
 use all five targets only where natural, 5–6 teacher pressure questions, and a closing
 30-second summarisation constraint.
7.	Wrap-up — 5 verbal retrieval questions (no notes) + each student produces one sentence
 about their actual job using one target.
8.	Homework — A 200–250-word professional statement using all five targets, with required
 content elements (thesis, evidence, differentiation, risk acknowledgement) and an additional
 60-second challenge.
9.	Five-minute review for the next lesson — Saved for Lesson [n+1]: Part 1 situation-to-
 expression retrieval (5 items), Part 2 completion prompts (5 sentence starters, 15–20 s
 each), Part 3 one-minute challenge (45-s response using ≥3 previous targets), and teacher
 assessment criteria.
Reference targets from Lesson 1 ("Presenting the Investment Case")
10.	build on — continuity from existing strengths
11.	set out — present reasoning in a clear, structured way
12.	Our investment case rests on… — establish the central thesis
13.	What sets us apart is… — demonstrate differentiation
14.	A compelling value proposition — explain why the investment merits consideration
